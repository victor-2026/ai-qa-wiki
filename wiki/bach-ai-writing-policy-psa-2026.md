## Summary  
James Bach’s recent Satisfice blog posts — *“Don’t Say You Use AI for Writing”* (June 2022) and *“Humans In The Way”* (May 2022) — lay out a concise stance on AI‑assisted authorship. Bach treats AI as a **thinking partner**, not a co‑author. The core message is that the person who signs a piece of text remains solely responsible for its content; claiming AI authorship is both inaccurate and ethically risky. To operationalise this view, he and his team published a **5‑point AI‑writing policy** that clarifies when AI assistance is permissible and how it must be disclosed.

---

## Key Concepts  

| Concept | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **AI as a thinking partner** | AI tools may be consulted for ideas, outlines, or phrasing, but the final prose is written and owned by the human author. |
| **Authorship = accountability** | Only a person can be held accountable for published statements. Attributing authorship to a tool erodes responsibility and can lead to scandals (e.g., the infamous “ChatGPT co‑author” incident). |
| **Tool vs. creator distinction** | Treating AI as a manager or creator is a category error; tools support human work but never replace the creator. |
| **5‑point AI‑writing policy** | 1️⃣ I am the author – AI only assists. 2️⃣ Substantial edits turn AI‑generated text into my own work; verbatim lifts remain non‑authored. 3️⃣ Pure prompting without personal contribution yields no credit. 4️⃣ No impersonation or false claim of ownership. 5️⃣ Disclose substantive AI involvement, especially when AI produces whole sections. |
| **Risk & reputation management** | Mis‑representing AI contribution can damage personal and organisational credibility and may breach legal or regulatory expectations (e.g., “Article 27 – accountable human behind AI content”). |

---

## Practical Applications  

1. **Writing Guidelines for Teams**  
   - Adopt the 5‑point policy as a standard clause in style guides.  
   - Require a short “AI‑assistance statement” in each document (e.g., “AI was used for brainstorming only”).  

2. **Code‑review & Test‑case Generation**  
   - When AI suggests test scenarios, the tester must rewrite or adapt them before inclusion, ensuring the final artefact is human‑authored.  

3. **Publication & Disclosure**  
   - For blogs, white‑papers, or conference submissions, embed a disclosure line if AI contributed more than a few sentences.  
   - Use version‑control comments to flag AI‑generated blocks for future reviewers.  

4. **Legal & Compliance Checks**  
   - Map the policy to regulatory frameworks that demand a named human accountable for AI‑generated content.  
   - Conduct periodic audits to verify that AI‑generated text is not presented as original author work.  

5. **Training & Culture**  
   - Run workshops illustrating the “thinking partner” model versus “author” model.  
   - Encourage transparent discussion of AI usage to avoid the “ghost‑writer” stigma.
