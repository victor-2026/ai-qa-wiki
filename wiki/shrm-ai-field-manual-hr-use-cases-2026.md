## SHRM AI Field Manual (2026) – 138 HR‑AI Use‑Case Risk/Cost Map  

**Source:** SHRM “AI Field Manual: 138 HR AI Use Cases as Risk/Cost Map” (2026). Survey of 1,722 HR professionals (Dec 2025) distilled >250 reported AI applications into 138 archetypes across 16 practice areas and placed them on a risk‑vs‑cost matrix.  

### Summary  
The manual groups HR‑AI use cases into four quadrants that combine **implementation cost** with **risk exposure**. The most common recommendation for high‑risk, low‑cost scenarios (the “Experimental” quadrant) is to run the technology in a **controlled sandbox** before any production rollout. The data show that 35 % of CHROs now view AI adoption as a major challenge, up from 29 % a year earlier, underscoring the need for a disciplined testing approach.

### Key Concepts  

| Quadrant (HR terminology) | Risk | Cost | Typical AI applications | Guidance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Low‑Hanging Fruit** | Low | Low | Standard HRIS analytics, chat‑bots for FAQs | Deploy broadly; monitor for minor errors. |
| **Experimental** | **High** | Low | Resume‑screening bots, voice‑assessment tools, generative interview assistants | **Confine to sandbox / controlled test environments**; treat as pilot projects. |
| **Strategic Bets** | Low | **High** | Advanced talent‑analytics platforms, AI‑driven workforce planning, custom L&D recommendation engines | Invest deliberately; pair with strong change‑management. |
| **Monitor** | **High** | **High** | Autonomous decision‑making in compliance, AI‑enabled compensation modeling, predictive attrition models | Hold back until industry best practices mature. |

#### HR‑X Dimensions (16 practice areas)  
1. **Functional Acceleration** – HR strategy, HR technology.  
2. **Market Discernment** – Recruiting, total rewards.  
3. **Organizational Impact** – Compliance, C‑suite/board liaison, future of work, org design & change.  
4. **Talent Optimization** – Employee experience, inclusion & diversity, labor relations, leadership development, L&D, performance, talent analytics, talent management.  

Each dimension intersects with the four quadrants, producing a **testing‑market map** that highlights where untested AI (e.g., “Gia‑style” resume screeners) is being deployed.

### Practical Applications  

- **Sandbox Pitch for HR Buyers** – Quote SHRM’s own advice: “high‑risk AI belongs in controlled test environments.” This language resonates with CHROs and can be used as a verification gate in procurement discussions (e.g., Stan, Evolve frameworks).  
- **Recruiting & Talent Analytics** – Focus pilot projects on the Experimental cells (resume parsing, candidate voice analysis). Use an open‑source suite such as **OrangeHRM** to simulate real workflows while keeping data isolated.  
- **Strategic Investment Planning** – Allocate budget to Strategic Bets that align with long‑term talent‑optimization goals (e.g., AI‑driven succession planning). Pair with robust governance to keep risk low.  
- **Monitoring High‑Risk Areas** – For Monitor‑quadrant uses (e.g., AI‑based compensation modeling), defer implementation until external standards, validation frameworks, or industry case studies emerge.  
- **Continuous Risk Mapping** – Re‑survey internal stakeholders annually to track shifts in perceived risk and cost, updating the quadrant placement as tools mature.
