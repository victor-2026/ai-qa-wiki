# QA Is Dead: Orchestrating Quality Across AI Engineering Teams  

**Authors:** Jay Aigner (CEO, JDAQA) & Ole Lensmar (CTO, Testkube)  
**Event:** September 17 2026 – Philadelphia Distilling, PA  

---

## Summary  
The presentation argues that traditional QA, as a separate discipline, can no longer keep pace with AI‑augmented development. While AI dramatically accelerates code creation, it does **not** own the four core aspects of quality: defining correctness, judging truth, assuming release risk, and handling unknown edge cases. Consequently, “quality debt” grows, stealing up to a third of an engineering year. The solution is an **Agentic SDLC** where validation is redistributed, humans retain ownership of the four non‑automatable decisions, and AI is used to surface information, draft artifacts, and increase test coverage.  

---

## Key Concepts  

| Concept | What It Means | Implications |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **Traditional SDLC → Quality Debt** | Steady velocity but accumulating defects over time. | Production incidents rise; rework consumes engineering capacity. |
| **AI‑augmented SDLC** | Code generation becomes cheap; validation costs rise (QA share up from 20 % → 35 %). | Development cost drops, but trust‑building moves to testing and review. |
| **Four Things AI Can’t Own** | 1. **Define correct** – setting the target. 2. **Determine truth** – acting as an oracle. 3. **Own the risk** – signing off releases. 4. **Face the unknown** – handling adversarial/edge cases. | Automation can *assist* but never replace human judgment in these areas. |
| **Agentic SDLC** | A future state where validation is the primary cost (QA 45 %, DevOps 12 %, Development 18 %). | Requirements and human‑centric decisions become the leverage point for scaling quality. |
| **Quality Debt Visualization** | ~110 days of a 365‑day year lost to rework and incident response. | Highlights urgency to restructure validation workflow. |
| **Human Footprint in Product Quality** | Humans decide on requirements, acceptance criteria, UX, ethics, accessibility, etc.; AI only drafts or flags. | Emphasizes a partnership model: AI surfaces, humans decide. |

---

## Practical Applications  

1. **Redesign Team Structure**  
   *Shift a larger portion of the engineering budget to validation roles.*  
   - Allocate dedicated “quality owners” responsible for the four non‑automatable decisions.  
   - Reduce pure development headcount; invest in cross‑functional analysts who bridge product, UX, and compliance.  

2. **Define Clear Hand‑Offs Between AI and Humans**  
   - **AI‑generated artifacts** (stories, specs, test checklists, UI mock‑ups) are **drafts**; humans must sign off the final version.  
   - Implement a “validation gate” where every AI output is reviewed against the four quality pillars before merging.  

3. **Leverage Agentic Testing Platforms**  
   - Use AI‑driven test orchestration to select, prioritize, and order test cases, but retain human oversight for edge‑case reasoning and risk acceptance.  

4. **Measure and Manage Quality Debt**  
   - Track days lost to rework/incident response as a KPI.  
   - Set targets to reduce the debt ratio (e.g., from 30 % to <15 % of engineering time) by increasing early‑stage validation.  

5. **Embed Ethics and Accessibility Early**  
   - Require human sign‑off on ethical considerations and accessibility compliance; AI can only flag WCAG basics.  

6. **Continuous Feedback Loop**  
   - Feed production incident data back into AI models to improve draft quality, but keep humans in the loop for interpreting root causes and updating requirements.  

By treating validation as the primary lever rather than an after‑thought, organizations can sustain high velocity while keeping quality debt under control.
