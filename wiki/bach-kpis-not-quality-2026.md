## This Fact About KPIs May Surprise You  
*James Bach – Satisfice blog (23 Aug 2026)*  

### Summary  
Bach argues that most KPI dashboards in software quality organisations measure **performance**, not **quality**. When a metric does not reflect the thing we truly care about, it can drive harmful behaviour and erode the very notion of quality. KPIs should be treated as **tools that support judgment**, not as substitutes for it. Before publishing a dashboard, ask whether the numbers actually capture quality or merely the speed, cost, or volume of work.

### Key Concepts  

| Concept | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Performance ≠ Quality** | High defect‑count reduction or faster releases may look good on a chart, but they do not guarantee that the product meets user needs or is robust. |
| **Metrics as Enablers** | Numbers provide data for discussion, risk assessment, and decision‑making, but they cannot replace the expert judgment of testers, developers, and product owners. |
| **Wrong‑Way KPI Selection** | Choosing a metric because it is easy to collect or pleases management often leads to “measurement tyranny” – teams optimise for the metric rather than for real quality. |
| **Judgment‑Centred Quality** | Quality remains a largely subjective assessment that involves context, risk, and stakeholder values; metrics should illuminate, not define, that assessment. |
| **KPI Fatigue** | In many testing cultures the term “KPI” has become a curse word, signalling a loss of trust in measurement as a proxy for quality. |

### Practical Applications  

1. **Audit Existing Dashboards**  
   - For each KPI ask: *What aspect of quality does this represent?*  
   - Remove or rename any metric that only reflects throughput, budget, or defect count without linking to user value or risk.  

2. **Design Quality‑Focused Metrics**  
   - Align metrics with **quality goals** (e.g., “percentage of critical user journeys covered by automated tests”, “mean time to detect a high‑severity defect in production”).  
   - Pair quantitative data with **qualitative checkpoints** (peer reviews, exploratory testing sessions).  

3. **Use Metrics as Conversation Starters**  
   - Present numbers alongside **contextual narratives**: why a spike occurred, what trade‑offs were made, and what the next steps are.  
   - Encourage teams to interpret the data rather than accept it as the final verdict.  

4. **Guard Against Metric Gaming**  
   - Rotate or diversify metrics periodically to prevent teams from “optimising to the metric”.  
   - Implement **review loops** where senior testers validate that metric trends align with real‑world quality observations.  

5. **Educate Stakeholders**  
   - Explain the distinction between performance indicators and quality indicators in meetings and reports.  
   - Provide concrete examples where a high‑performing KPI masked a quality regression.  

By treating KPIs as **enablers of judgment** rather than **substitutes for judgment**, organisations can preserve the meaning of “quality” while still benefiting from data‑driven insight.
