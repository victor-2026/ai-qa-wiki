## ISO 13485 – Quality Management System for Medical Devices  

**Summary**  
ISO 13485 is the international standard that defines a quality‑management system (QMS) specifically for medical‑device manufacturers. While it shares the structure of ISO 9001, it adds mandatory requirements for regulatory compliance, risk management (ISO 14971), traceability, and post‑market surveillance. Compliance is required for CE‑marking under the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR) and is recognised by the U.S. FDA as equivalent to 21 CFR 820.

---

### Key Concepts  

#### 1. Scope & Distinguishing Features  
| Aspect | ISO 9001 | ISO 13485 |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Primary aim | Customer satisfaction | Patient safety & regulatory compliance |
| Risk handling | General | Integrated ISO 14971 risk management |
| Traceability | Recommended | Mandatory for all records |
| Validation | Generic | Specific for processes, software, design |
| CAPA | Suggested | Required and documented |

#### 2. Core Clauses (high‑level view)  

| Clause | Main Requirements |
|--------|-------------------|
| **4 – QMS** | Documented quality policy, quality manual, strict control of documents and records (4.2.4). |
| **5 – Management Responsibility** | Top‑management commitment, communication of policy, periodic management review (5.6) with inputs such as audit results, CAPA status, and customer feedback. |
| **6 – Resource Management** | Competence & training, adequate infrastructure, controlled work environment (cleanrooms, labs). |
| **7 – Product Realisation** | Planning of quality activities, customer‑requirement review, full design‑development cycle (inputs → outputs → review → verification → validation → transfer) with a **Design History File (DHF)**, supplier control, validated production processes (e.g., sterilisation), and equipment calibration. |
| **8 – Measurement, Analysis & Improvement** | Customer feedback, internal audits, monitoring of processes/products, handling of non‑conforming items, data analysis, and a mandatory **CAPA** system (root‑cause analysis, effectiveness verification). |

#### 3. Frequently Audited Records  

- Quality Manual (4.2.2) – current, controlled version.  
- DHF (7.3) – complete evidence of every design stage.  
- Design Review minutes (7.3.4) – participants, decisions, actions.  
- Verification & Validation reports (7.3.6‑7.3.7).  
- Risk Management File (ISO 14971) – hazard‑control‑verification traceability.  
- CAPA files (8.5.2) – root cause, corrective action, effectiveness check.  
- Internal audit reports (8.2.4) and Management Review minutes (5.6).  
- Training & supplier audit records (6.2, 7.4).  
- Calibration certificates (7.6).

#### 4. Integration with Other Standards  

| Standard | Role in ISO 13485 |
|----------|-------------------|
| ISO 14971 | Mandatory risk‑management process. |
| IEC 62304 | Software lifecycle compliance. |
| IEC 62366 | Usability‑engineering requirements. |
| 21 CFR 820 | U.S. QSR, harmonised with ISO 13485 (2026 update). |
| EU MDR | Legal framework that mandates ISO 13485 for CE‑marking. |

### Practical Applications  

#### Implementing a QMS for a Bio‑informatics Platform (example)  
1. **Define a DHF for software** – capture requirements, architecture, code reviews, verification tests, and validation against clinical use cases.  
2. **Integrate ISO 14971** – create a risk file linking each identified hazard to controls, verification activities, and residual‑risk acceptance.  
3. **Establish CAPA** – monitor model‑drift alerts, perform root‑cause analysis, apply corrective updates, and verify effectiveness through post‑deployment data.  
4. **Maintain an audit trail** – use electronic record‑keeping (21 CFR Part 11) to ensure every data transformation is traceable and retrievable.  
5. **Continuous readiness** – schedule internal audits, update training matrices, review CAPA trends quarterly, and feed results into management review.

#### Ensuring Audit Readiness  
- **Document control**: version‑controlled manuals, SOPs, and records stored in a secure, searchable repository.  
- **Training matrix**: map competencies to tasks; record completion and re‑qualification dates.  
- **CAPA trend analysis**: plot frequency, severity, and closure time; demonstrate systematic improvement.  
- **Management review**: produce minutes that capture all required inputs (audit outcomes, customer complaints, process performance) and documented decisions.
