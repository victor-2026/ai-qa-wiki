# Evolving Quality with AI Testing — Community Reflection

> Ministry of Testing Moments, Sep 8, 2026. Author: Rosie Sherry (CEO & Founder, Ministry of Testing).

A reflection on AI testing tools: the author wants to explore all of them but doesn't have time. The core argument is that we need to talk honestly about what hasn't worked in test automation, not just success stories.

## Core Thesis
> "A part of me wants to quit my CEO day job and spend time exploring all the AI testing tools. It feels like such an interesting time to explore how to do quality better."

## The Uncomfortable Truths
1. **Test automation hasn't failed, but the conversation has been biased** toward success stories
2. **Those who haven't succeeded with test automation** — their experience gets lost
3. **"Yes, that test automation practice seems really interesting, but that's not how we do it"** — common behind-the-scenes reality
4. **QA often doesn't own test automation** — developers do
5. **Quality roles split with support or product** — the boundaries are blurring

## Key Insight
> "Talks and articles are normally biased towards success stories. But it's important we talk more openly about these things, when we can clearly see that certain practices aren't helpful, then we can come together to problem solve for better ways."

> "The models need to be rewritten, and the ones that are going to work are the ones we create for ourselves, for our own systems, not one that is preached down upon us."

> "What feels clearer by the day, is that everything is changing, it is up to us to create a better quality engineering industry, which I believe we can do."

## Connection to Our Work
- **William Tran's "test doesn't catch bugs"** → Rosie agrees, the bias is real
- **Quality gaps** → systemic issue, not individual failure
- **Test automation fatigue** → many QA gave up after failed attempts
- **The shift-left metaphor** → career resilience = quality engineering
