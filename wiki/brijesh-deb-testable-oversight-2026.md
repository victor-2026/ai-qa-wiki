## Six questions (who/when/what/competence/authority/stop)

1. Who is the human? 2. At what point do they intervene? 3. What information do they receive? 4. Do they understand enough to challenge? 5. Authority to override? 6. Can they stop before irreversibility? NIST AI RMF 1.0 framing praised (roles, documented processes, accountability, competence) — with caveat: published Jan 2023, under revision, foundation not final word.

## APPROVE-button critique

Payment-proposal scenario with 5 checks (why recommended? evidence visible? policy understood? authorized? pre-consequence intervention?). Taxonomy: observation (sees what happened) vs incident response (reacts after) vs oversight (none without info+authority). Testing thesis: **test whether the human-AI control system works when AI misbehaves** — recognize plausible-wrong? challenge vs defer? intervene in time? actually stop? "Human oversight should itself be testable." Closer: "Without that, human oversight remains a principle, not yet a control."
