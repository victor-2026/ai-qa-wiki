## Valerii K. (ODDITY) – Opus 5.5 Upgrade Validation Checklist (2026‑09‑25)

**Author:** Valerii K., Director QA, ODDITY  
**Purpose:** Provide a reusable, workload‑centric protocol for validating large‑model upgrades (e.g., Opus 5 → Opus 5.5, Qwen 2.5 → Qwen 3, Gemma variants).  
**Scope:** The checklist is intended for internal QA teams that must compare new model releases against existing baselines while keeping effort settings (temperature, prompt format, etc.) identical.

---

### Summary
The checklist rejects leaderboard‑only validation and instead measures **real‑world task success** on the team’s own workloads. Five dimensions are evaluated for each workload:

| Dimension | What to measure | Why it matters |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| **Task Completion** | % of tasks finished correctly | Core functional parity |
| **Tool Calls & Fallback** | Correctness of tool invocation, graceful degradation when tools fail | Guarantees reliable augmentation (e.g., code execution, web‑search) |
| **Cost per Successful Task** | Monetary cost normalized to *successful* tasks (not per token) | Reveals true economic impact of the upgrade |
| **Latency & Token Usage** | End‑to‑end response time and token count per task | Affects user experience and scaling budget |
| **Evidence Quality + Human Review** | Rating of output evidence (citations, reasoning) plus reviewer confidence | Ensures outputs remain trustworthy for downstream decisions |

The author notes an **effort‑parity rule**: benchmarks must be run with identical temperature, prompt templates, and other “effort settings” to avoid skewed comparisons. Preliminary, unvalidated runs suggest Opus 5.5 can be ~40 % cheaper per successful task than Opus 5.

---

### Key Concepts

- **Workload‑Centric Validation** – Use the team’s actual prompts, data, and toolchain rather than generic benchmark suites.
- **Effort‑Parity Rule** – Freeze all non‑model variables (temperature, max‑tokens, prompt style) when swapping models; otherwise cost and quality differences are conflated.
- **Cost‑per‑SUCCESSFUL‑Task** – Shifts the economic denominator from “per token” to “per task that meets acceptance criteria,” aligning measurement with business outcomes.
- **Tool‑Call Fidelity** – New models often change how they invoke external tools; the checklist explicitly tracks correct calls and graceful fallback paths.
- **Human‑in‑the‑Loop Review** – Quantitative metrics are complemented by qualitative human assessment of evidence quality, preserving the “trust but verify” principle.

---

### Practical Applications

1. **Model Migration Playbooks** – Embed the five‑row checklist into any internal SOP for swapping LLM providers or versions.  
2. **Cross‑Model Cost Modeling** – Use the cost‑per‑successful‑task metric to forecast budget impact when scaling from pilot to production.  
3. **Tool‑Integration Regression Tests** – Automate checks that verify tool‑call schemas (e.g., function‑calling JSON) remain stable after an upgrade.  
4. **Latency‑SLA Monitoring** – Tie latency results to service‑level agreements; flag regressions before they affect end‑users.  
5. **Evidence Audits** – Pair the checklist with the “AI QA Evidence Layer” framework to record human review scores and maintain audit trails for compliance.  
6. **Continuous Prompt Evaluation** – Combine with Kiro’s 4‑stage live‑signal methodology to detect prompt‑drift caused by model upgrades.  

By treating the checklist as a **radar, not an endorsement**, teams can adopt a disciplined, data‑driven stance on model upgrades while preserving flexibility to experiment with novel architectures.
