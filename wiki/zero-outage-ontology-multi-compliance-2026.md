## ZOIS Opinion Paper – Ontology‑Enhanced Multi‑Compliance Capability  
*Antal Futó, 23 September 2026*  

### Summary  
The paper argues that the exploding number of regulatory, contractual and supply‑chain obligations can no longer be managed with traditional taxonomies that rely on human navigation. Building on the ESARIS security pillar, ZOIS proposes to evolve the existing taxonomy into a **formal ontology**: a machine‑readable model that defines controls, assets, risks, services, suppliers, processes and requirements together with their explicit inter‑relationships.  

By describing an organization’s architecture once in this shared ontology, the same description can be **mapped automatically to many compliance frameworks** (e.g., NIS2, ISO 27001, GDPR). This “describe‑once / map‑many” approach yields traceability, reduces duplication, and enables automated analysis, decision support and AI‑driven governance. The paper also stresses that the real value lies in **industry‑wide consensus on concepts and terminology**, not in a particular technology stack.  

A dedicated **quality‑assurance regime** for the ontology—combining expert reviews, consistency checks, relationship verification and periodic reality‑fit assessments—is presented as a counterpart to ZOIS’s golden‑dataset maintenance doctrine.

### Key Concepts  

| Concept | Meaning & Relevance |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Ontology vs. Taxonomy** | A taxonomy groups items hierarchically; an ontology adds formal definitions and explicit relations, making the model executable by machines. |
| **Multi‑Compliance Mapping** | One canonical architecture model is linked to the control sets of multiple standards, enabling simultaneous compliance verification. |
| **Traceability as a First‑Class Property** | Every control, risk, asset and requirement is linked back to its source regulation, evidence artifact and responsible party, supporting independent auditability. |
| **QA‑of‑Ontology** | Beyond syntax validation, the ontology undergoes expert peer review, automated consistency checks, and periodic alignment with the evolving operational reality. |
| **Collaborative Development** | ZOIS acts as a neutral facilitator; members contribute domain knowledge, while the shared model remains open for extension and partner interoperability. |
| **Digital‑Twin Outlook** | The ontology can serve as the knowledge backbone for a live digital twin of an organization’s compliance posture, feeding AI‑driven risk analytics. |

### Practical Applications  

1. **Automated Compliance Audits** – Compliance tools ingest the ontology and automatically generate evidence matrices for each target framework, cutting manual effort.  
2. **Decision Support for Risk Management** – AI agents query the ontology to assess the impact of a new service on existing controls, surfacing gaps before deployment.  
3. **Supply‑Chain Assurance** – Supplier data modeled in the ontology enables rapid propagation of regulatory changes (e.g., NIS2 updates) across the entire value chain.  
4. **Evidence Re‑use** – A single verification artifact (test report, configuration snapshot) can be linked to multiple compliance gates, mirroring ZOIS’s “evidence‑pack” philosophy.  
5. **Continuous QA Loop** – The ontology’s QA process mirrors the golden‑dataset lifecycle: periodic expert review → automated validation → score‑based fitness reporting → corrective updates.  
6. **Inter‑Organizational Interoperability** – Partners adopting the shared ontology can exchange compliance artefacts without re‑mapping, facilitating joint audits and joint ventures.  

---

### Limitations  

* The paper is an opinion piece; it does not present empirical performance data.  
* Cost figures (e.g., average breach cost $4.44 M) are cited from external sources, not derived from ZOIS research.  
* Adoption requires substantial initial modelling effort and cultural alignment around shared terminology.
