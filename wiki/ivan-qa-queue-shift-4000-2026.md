## Ivan Davidov – QA Queue‑Shift Report (4 000 Engineers, 2026‑09‑25)

**Author:** Ivan Davidov – Agentic Quality Engineer, ArchQA founder, Agentic Playwright (Bulgaria)  
**Source:** LinkedIn post (user‑pasted, 5 h old). The underlying formal report has not yet been indexed.  

### Summary
The post presents early‑stage empirical data on how AI‑driven tooling has moved – but not eliminated – the bottleneck in modern QA pipelines. Among 4 000 engineers, a larger share of features now reach testing earlier, yet the QA queue has grown longer and more iteration‑heavy. The author attributes the core problem to “questions after the code” and proposes a pre‑prompt gate (clear acceptance criteria) as the most effective mitigation (77 % of respondents).  

### Key Concepts
| Concept | Insight |
|---|---|
| **Queue‑shift metrics** | • 65 % of features arrive at testing sooner.<br>• 55 % report a longer QA queue.<br>• 52 % experience additional fix‑retest cycles.<br>• 47 % work in the primary scenario but break elsewhere.<br>• 44 % suffer from unexamined dependencies. |
| **Toll‑booth metaphor** | Adding a “fast lane” (automation) speeds individual cars but leaves the single inspection window as the limiting factor, making the line overall longer. |
| **Timing of clarification** | 56 % of engineers spend extra time clarifying feature intent *after* code is written, i.e., on “ready for QA” tickets. This delays feedback and inflates the queue. |
| **Acceptance criteria as binary gate** | 77 % of teams mitigate the shift by defining crystal‑clear, binary acceptance criteria (a “done‑definition”). The author likens this to the 2005 agile poster that reduced ambiguity. |
| **“Questions before the prompt”** | The recommended “step‑0” gate: resolve functional questions **before** the AI prompt or PR is generated, rather than after. A second reviewer at the booth cannot compensate for upstream uncertainty. |

### Practical Applications
1. **Cost‑of‑delayed‑verification arguments** – Use the 65/55/52/47/44 percentages to quantify the hidden expense of late‑stage defect discovery when building leadership cases for early‑stage verification investments.  
2. **Step‑0 relevance gate** – Implement a mandatory pre‑prompt checklist (e.g., “What is the exact behavior? What are edge cases?”) in CI pipelines. This aligns with risk‑tiered testing frameworks that require a “relevance” assessment before AI‑generated code is submitted.  
3. **Spec‑driven/ATDD tooling demand** – The 77 % mitigation figure signals strong market demand for tools that automatically generate and enforce binary acceptance criteria (e.g., Valentina, CIGE stable‑intent). Prioritize integration of such tooling in test‑authoring suites.  
4. **Dependency visibility** – Since 44 % of features suffer from unseen dependencies, embed automated dependency‑impact analysis (static call‑graph, runtime tracing) early in the development flow.  
5. **Iterative refinement workflow** – Shift refinement conversations from post‑code “ready for QA” tickets to pre‑coding design reviews, reducing the 56 % time spent on retroactive clarification.
