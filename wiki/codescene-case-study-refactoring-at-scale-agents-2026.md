## CodeScene – Refactoring at Scale with AI Agents  
*Street Fighter III 3rd Strike (≈300 K LOC), 2026‑09‑24*  

**Author:** Adam Tornhill (CodeScene) – execution by Daniel Webb & Markus Borg; enterprise delivery by NeoSee  

### Summary  
CodeScene applied a fully‑automated, agent‑driven refactoring pipeline to the 300 k‑line C codebase of *Street Fighter III 3rd Strike*. Over three weeks (part‑time) the system performed 2 903 commits, touching 726 files and modifying 252 k lines. By iteratively feeding the **CodeHealth MCP server** – a deterministic quality metric – back into large‑language‑model agents (Claude Opus proved most effective), the team lifted the CodeHealth score from 5.6 to the target 10.0. Correctness was guaranteed through a **replay‑trace harness** that compared frame‑by‑frame state hashes, ensuring the game behaved identically after each change. The effort cost roughly $4 K in token usage, a fraction of the 12–18 months a human expert would need.

### Key Concepts  

| Concept | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **CodeHealth MCP Server** | An objective, validated metric (healthy code → 10× faster evolution, 15× fewer defects). | Provides a deterministic feedback loop that agents can optimise without drifting. |
| **Replay‑trace equivalence harness** | Generates a golden hash for each game frame; after refactoring, the same hash must be reproduced. | Guarantees behavioral equivalence even when large structural changes are made. |
| **Iterative Playbook** | Agents propose refactoring “recipes” with pre‑conditions; successful recipes are recorded, failures are kept as negative examples. | Mirrors mutation‑testing style learning; the playbook evolves into a domain‑specific rule set. |
| **AI‑discovered domain rules** | Novel patterns such as *Shared Index Range*, *Action Parameter*, and *Uniform Step Table* that differ from classic Fowler refactorings. | Demonstrates that agents can surface hidden, code‑base‑specific abstractions that humans may overlook. |
| **Economic token efficiency** | $4 K token spend yielded ~70 % fewer AI‑induced defects and ~45 % less token waste on future features. | Shows that a validated metric makes large‑scale remediation financially viable. |

### Practical Applications  

1. **Deterministic Gate Doctrine** – Use an independent quality signal (e.g., CodeHealth) as a “judge” that steers code‑generation agents, mirroring the verdict‑layer approach in AI‑QA pipelines.  
2. **Behavioral‑Equivalence Testing** – Adopt replay‑trace or state‑hash harnesses for domains where deterministic execution is possible (games, simulations, embedded control loops).  
3. **Playbook‑with‑Failures** – Record both successful and failed refactorings; the negative examples act as guardrails for future agents, similar to mutation‑matrix techniques.  
4. **Domain‑Specific Rule Extraction** – Let agents discover patterns unique to a codebase, then codify them for future automated maintenance or onboarding guides.  
5. **Economic Justification** – Quantify token spend against projected defect reduction and development speed gains to secure stakeholder buy‑in.  

### Limitations & Caveats  

* The study focuses on a single, highly deterministic codebase; many enterprise systems lack a replay‑trace harness.  
* $4 K token cost excludes human time spent designing the playbook and setting up the harness.  
* Results are vendor‑specific and may not generalise without similar metric validation (e.g., Code Red, AI‑Ready‑Code).  

### Outlook  

A follow‑up study at Lund University will compare student productivity when extending the “healthy” versus the original code version, providing controlled evidence of the long‑term benefits of AI‑guided refactoring.
