## VP of AI Quality Engineering (AIQE) – A Blueprint for Safe, Competitive AI  

**Author:** James Bach – *Satisfice Blog* (22 Sep 2026)  

### Summary  
Large enterprises that ignore disciplined AI governance risk losing market relevance or exposing themselves to costly failures. Bach proposes a dedicated executive function – **Vice‑President of AI Governance / AI Quality Engineering (VP‑AIQE)** – backed by a compact analyst team. The unit would continuously scan AI trends, run controlled experiments, and embed risk‑aware practices across IT and R&D. By reporting risks, benefits, and compliance status to senior leadership, the VP‑AIQE creates the “small measure of bureaucracy” needed for safe AI adoption while preserving agility.

### Key Concepts  

| Concept | What it Means | Why it Matters |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| **AI Governance** | Formal oversight of AI strategy, policy, and regulatory interaction. | Aligns AI initiatives with corporate risk appetite and emerging law. |
| **AI Quality Engineering** | Systematic testing, validation, and monitoring of AI models and data pipelines. | Guarantees that AI outputs meet functional, ethical, and safety standards. |
| **Analyst Team** | A lean group (3‑5) that tracks external AI research, runs pilot experiments, and collects telemetry. | Provides the evidence base for decision‑making without bloating the organization. |
| **Risk‑Aware Experimentation** | Controlled trials in IT/R&D that surface both opportunities and failure modes. | Turns “wild‑west” tinkering into learnable, repeatable processes. |
| **Regulatory Liaison** | Ongoing dialogue with authorities, preparing for future AI‑specific rules. | Prevents surprise compliance costs and reputational damage. |
| **Human‑Centred Authority** | The VP must have a public reputation for “speaking truth to power” and a background in critical software testing. | Ensures that technical risk is communicated candidly to executives. |

### Practical Applications  

1. **Establish the VP‑AIQE Office**  
   * Report directly to the C‑suite (e.g., CTO or COO).  
   * Secure a modest budget for tooling, data‑science licences, and analyst headcount.  

2. **Define Core Responsibilities**  
   * **Trend Surveillance:** Weekly briefs on new models, datasets, and emerging hazards.  
   * **Experimentation Framework:** A sandbox where AI prototypes are evaluated against predefined safety criteria (bias, drift, robustness).  
   * **Company‑Wide Monitoring:** Deploy telemetry (e.g., model‑level logs, usage metrics) to a central dashboard accessible to senior leadership.  
   * **Compliance & Reporting:** Maintain an up‑to‑date matrix of applicable regulations; produce quarterly risk‑benefit reports for the board.  

3. **Integrate with Existing Teams**  
   * **IT/R&D Collaboration:** Embed AIQE analysts in product squads to co‑design guardrails (e.g., input validation, output throttling).  
   * **Testing Culture Alignment:** Leverage the organization’s testing mindset (RST, exploratory testing) to evaluate AI behaviours beyond traditional unit tests.  

4. **Metrics & Guardrails**  
   * **Safety KPI:** Percentage of AI releases passing the AIQE risk‑assessment checklist.  
   * **Cost KPI:** Token spend per validated business outcome.  
   * **Compliance KPI:** Number of regulatory gaps identified vs. resolved each quarter.  

5. **Communication & Education**  
   * Publish internal “AI Risk Briefs” that translate technical findings into business language.  
   * Conduct workshops on responsible AI use, reinforcing the “human moat” concept from the Principles of Responsible Work v2.0.  

6. **Scalable Governance Model**  
   * Start with a pilot in a high‑impact domain (e.g., customer‑facing recommendation engine).  
   * Iterate the governance process, then roll out to additional AI initiatives.  

### Benefits  

* **Strategic Safety:** Early detection of model failures, bias, or regulatory breaches.  
* **Financial Discipline:** Clear visibility into token spend versus realized value.  
* **Reputational Shield:** Proactive compliance reduces the likelihood of public scandals.  
* **Cultural Alignment:** Embeds a testing‑first mindset into AI development, preserving the organization’s quality heritage.
