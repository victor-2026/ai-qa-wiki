## Alex Karp (Palantir) - Two Types ChatGPT Won't Replace

**Summary**
Palantir CEO Alex Karp (Business Insider interview, 2026) names two categories safe from AI replacement: 1) **Hands workers** - artisans, welders, electricians - professional skill that cannot be entrusted to a soulless machine; 2) **Neurodivergent** - ADHD, autism, dyslexia - people who physically cannot think in templates, invent non-standard. Neural nets work perfectly by instruction; neurodivergent cannot make it perfect but can invent something genuinely new. Palantir runs a dedicated neurodivergent hiring program; Karp calls his own dyslexia his main career advantage. Irony: what classic education punished for decades - scattered, chaotic, can't do as told - became market advantage in 2026.

---

## Key Points

| Category | Why safe | Karp logic |
|----------|----------|------------|
| **Hands / artisans** | Physical skill, embodiment, real-world manipulation | AI is instruction-bound, not embodied |
| **Neurodivergent** | Cannot think by template, invents non-standard | Nets are perfect instruction followers; value is in non-standard invention |

**Palantir action:** Dedicated neurodivergent hiring program; Karp frames dyslexia as competitive edge.

## Our analysis (for Victor)

1. **Maps to exploratory vs checking (Bach/RST).** Hands = physical checking that AI can't embody; neurodivergent = exploratory testing that breaks templates. Automation (Mark Paemaa's confidence suite) covers the template, neurodivergent thinking covers the edge that template misses. Both survive because they are not templatable.

2. **Supports Article 27 - Guided QA Engineer as non-template role.** QA promoted from re-running checks to investigating risks and questioning what software is supposed to do (Steven Clark's point). That questioning is exactly the non-template invention Karp values - dyslexic/ADHD pattern-breaking is an asset for finding what the spec didn't anticipate.

3. **Hiring implication for Victor's Quality Operating Model.** If Palantir bets on neurodivergent for innovation, QA hiring should screen for divergent thinking, not just process compliance. For Appfire/HTEC QA leadership: build teams with both - reliable checkers (automation + process) and divergent explorers - the 5x securing cost (Kabanov) is paid when everyone thinks alike.

4. **School punishment -> market advantage is the same as QA history.** Classic QA was punished for "not following the plan, being chaotic" - now that chaos is the premium in an AI-saturated market. Mirrors the shift from `passionate about quality` noise to specific divergent skill.
