## Why E2E Tests Change – Insights from 16 K Commits (Konstantin Slavnov, 2024‑2026)

**Source:** Konstantin Slavnov (Qure / JetBrains InnoHub) – “Why E2E Tests Change” (post 23 h old, 25 reactions, 25 comments) + research site <https://quretests.com/research/e2e-changes> + HekaJev classifier <https://github.com/zurk/hekajev> (MIT).  
**Scope:** 549 224 commits across 21 public repositories (IBM, Mozilla, Red Hat, Automattic, Snowflake, Supabase …) → 16 024 commits identified as E2E‑test changes.  

---

### Summary  

The study classifies every E2E‑test commit into *new coverage* (adding or expanding tests) or *maintenance* (repair, refactor, flakiness fix, etc.). Overall, 45 % of the work is upkeep, while 38 % expands coverage; the remainder is “unknown”.  

* **2026 surge:** New‑coverage share rose from 34.8 % (Jan 2024) to 47.7 % (Jan‑Sep 2026) in most projects, hinting at a possible AI‑driven boost, though causality is unproven.  
* **Flakiness:** Repairs grew from 7.9 % to 10.0 % of E2E commits, appearing in 11 of 13 repos. Roughly one‑third of newly added test files need a follow‑up change within 30 days, and 28 % of flakiness fixes trigger another flakiness change in the same file.  
* **AI attribution:** 805 commits flagged as AI‑generated show a +5.69 pp coverage lift, but the 95 % confidence interval includes zero, so the effect cannot be claimed statistically.  
* **Age effect:** The proportion of E2E commits grows with project age (0.8 % in the first year → 5.1 % after twelve years).  

The author treats the analysis as exploratory, not an industry benchmark, and supplies the HekaJev tool for reproducibility.
