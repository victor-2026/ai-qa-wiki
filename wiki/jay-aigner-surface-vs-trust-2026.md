## Jay Aigner (JDAQA) – “Surface Area vs. Trust: Two Different Jobs” (2026‑09‑24)

### Summary  
In a recent LinkedIn post, Jay Aigner (CEO of JDAQA) argues that AI‑driven quality engineering is being forced to juggle two mutually exclusive responsibilities: **expanding the testing surface** and **certifying what can be trusted**. He contends that conflating the two creates a “wall” that yields massive output (≈10× the productivity of a human engineer) but delivers no reliable verdicts. The only sustainable path, he says, is to **separate the jobs**—let automation generate breadth while humans retain the authority to sign off on safety, risk, and accountability. Aigner presented this thesis at a Philadelphia engineering‑leaders round‑table co‑hosted with Ole Lensmar (CTO, Testkube), signalling a growing alignment between JDAQA and Testkube on the “verdict layer” concept.

### Key Concepts  

| Concept | Meaning | Implication |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Surface‑area expansion** | AI agents can explore code paths, generate tests, and simulate scenarios at a scale far beyond a single engineer (≈10× output). | Provides rapid feedback and early defect detection, but the results are raw data without judgment. |
| **Trust certification** | Determining which findings are safe to act on (green‑light, risky, reject). This requires human authority, name‑on‑incident‑report, and 2 am accountability. | Guarantees that automated insights translate into reliable releases; cannot be fully delegated to a model. |
| **Verdict layer** | A dedicated decision‑making tier that consumes the surface‑area output and issues trusted verdicts. | Acts as a firewall between massive automation and production, preventing “zero‑trust” releases. |
| **Universal wall (10× output, zero trusted lines)** | The failure mode when the two jobs are merged: high‑volume signals but no confidence in any of them. | Leads to wasted effort, potential regressions, and loss of stakeholder trust. |
| **Separation of jobs** | Winners (e.g., JDAQA, Testkube) keep the verdict layer human‑centric while allowing AI to handle breadth. | Delivers both speed and reliability, creating a competitive advantage. |
| **Accountability framing** | References to incident‑report sign‑off and 2 am on‑call duties emphasize who ultimately owns the verdict. | Provides a concrete metric for “Track 3” (who signs the verdict) in product governance. |
| **Strategic partnership** | Joint appearances by Aigner and Lensmar reinforce a shared vision for a split‑responsibility architecture. | Signals market momentum toward integrated but distinct AI‑QA components. |

### Practical Applications  

1. **Leadership communication** – Use the “10× output, zero trust” line to illustrate the risk of over‑automation in board or executive briefings.  
2. **Tool‑selection framework** – Prioritize solutions that expose a clear verdict layer (e.g., Qodo’s independent examiner, Radik’s immutable gate) rather than pure surface‑area generators.  
3. **Process design** – Structure CI pipelines so that AI‑generated tests feed into a human‑review checkpoint that signs off on release readiness.  
4. **Accountability contracts** – Embed incident‑report sign‑off clauses and 2 am on‑call responsibilities into service‑level agreements for AI‑QA components.  
5. **Collaboration roadmaps** – Track joint initiatives between JDAQA and Testkube; co‑develop APIs that hand off surface data to a shared verdict service.  
6. **Risk‑based coverage** – Combine surface expansion with risk‑prioritization layers (e.g., OrangePro) to focus human review on high‑impact findings.
