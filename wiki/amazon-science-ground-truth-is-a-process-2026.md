# Amazon Science: Ground Truth Is a Process, Not a Dataset  
*June 3 2026 – V. Saligrama (Amazon Scholar, BU) et al.*

## Summary
When evaluating factuality in long AI‑generated research reports, the traditional “one‑shot labeling” approach fails: PhD specialists achieved only 60 % accuracy on hidden, pre‑validated claims. By turning the benchmark into an **audit‑then‑score** loop—where challengers submit evidence against a human‑written rationale and auditors adjudicate—the same experts raised accuracy to **90 %** after four rounds. The study demonstrates that “ground truth” should be treated as an evolving process, not a static dataset, and proposes a reusable architecture (DeepFact‑Eval) for fact‑checking agents.

## Key Concepts  

| Concept | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| **Audit‑then‑Score** | A two‑stage protocol: a challenger disputes a benchmark answer with concrete evidence; an auditor compares the challenger’s evidence to the original rationale and decides the outcome. | Turns labeling into a dispute‑resolution task, dramatically improving reliability. |
| **Challenger vs. Auditor** | *Challenger* – model or human that provides counter‑evidence; *Auditor* – expert who validates the dispute without re‑labeling from scratch. | Separates evidence generation from judgment, leveraging expertise efficiently. |
| **Ground‑Truth Process** | Ground truth is continuously refined through cycles of challenges, audits, and revisions rather than a frozen collection of labels. | Keeps benchmarks aligned with evolving knowledge and reduces hidden‑bias errors. |
| **DeepFact‑Eval Architecture** | Agent reads full report, plans literature searches, summarizes retrieved documents, asks follow‑up questions, and finally issues a verdict with explanation. | Provides a template for building fact‑checking agents that operate under the audit‑then‑score paradigm. |
| **Golden‑Dataset Doctrine** | Version 0 seeds the benchmark; each subsequent audit adds a new version with stored rationales and evidence artifacts. | Enables traceable, versioned benchmarks that can be re‑used and audited over time. |

## Practical Applications  

1. **Benchmark Design** – Adopt the audit‑then‑score workflow when creating fact‑checking or factuality benchmarks, especially for long‑context, cross‑document tasks.  
2. **Human Resource Allocation** – Deploy experts as **Auditors** (high‑accuracy dispute resolution) rather than as primary labelers, cutting labeling cost while improving quality.  
3. **Model Evaluation Pipeline** – Integrate the DeepFact‑Eval loop:  
   - *Context ingestion* → *search planning* → *document summarization* → *interactive clarification* → *verdict + rationale*.  
   This structure can be reused for other verification‑heavy domains (e.g., policy analysis, medical literature synthesis).  
4. **Evidence‑Centric Data Management** – Store each label together with its original rationale and any challenger evidence; treat these bundles as immutable evidence artifacts for future audits.  
5. **Continuous Benchmark Calibration** – Schedule periodic audit rounds (e.g., quarterly) to capture emerging knowledge, resolve ambiguities, and keep the benchmark relevant as models improve.  

## Implications for AI Evaluation  

- **Model‑Benchmark Discord** is not automatically a model failure; ambiguous or incomplete benchmarks can inflate error rates.  
- **Evolving Infrastructure**: As AI reaches expert‑level performance, static datasets become insufficient. Ongoing human‑model collaboration is required to maintain trustworthy evaluation standards.  
- **Attestor Economics**: Auditing disputed claims yields ≈ 90 % correctness versus ≈ 60 % for fresh labeling, suggesting a cost‑effective strategy for large‑scale QA pipelines.
