These names generally fall into four distinct categories:
1. Relatable Human Names 

Giving an agent a human name helps build personal connection and familiarity
- **Classic Names**: Common, friendly choices like **Alex**, **Sam**, **Henry**, **Mia**, or **Ava**.
- **"Gentle" Professional Names**: Names like **Claude** are chosen for their non-threatening, scholarly feel (reminiscent of Claude Monet or Debussy).
- **Gender-Neutral Choices**: To avoid stereotypes, many modern names are gender-neutral, such as **Kai**, **Skye**, **Sage**, **Jordan**, or **Taylor**.
2. Functional & Role-Based Names

These names focus on what the agent actually does, providing immediate clarity for the user.
- **Direct Roles**: Titles like **Navigator**, **Advisor**, **Tutor**, or **Concierge**.
- **Compound Names**: Combining a goal with a suffix, such as **ShopMate**, **FitBuddy**, **TalentFinder**, or **BudgetBot**.
- **Technical Identifiers**: Prefixes like **AI-** or **Duo-** (e.g., AI-Reviewer) are often used in corporate settings to distinguish between humans and bots.
3. Futuristic & Tech-Centric Names

These emphasize innovation, intelligence, and high-speed processing.
- **Abstract Concepts**: **Nova**, **Aurora**, **Zenith**, **Vector**, **Aura**, or **Nexus**.
- **Tech References**: Names like **Byte**, **Pixel**, **Cypher**, **Helix**, or **Axon**.
- **Scientific Pioneers**: Naming agents after historical figures like Ada (Lovelace), Turing, or Pascal.
4. Playful & Creative Names

Mascots or pun-based names inject humor and personality into the brand.
- **Puns**: **Bot-ilda**, **ByteMe**, **Sir Talks-a-Lot**, or **ChattyMcBot**.
- **Cute/Mascot Style**: **Zuzu**, **Pip**, **Dottie**, **Bubbles**, or **BuddyBee**.
- Notable Industry Examples: 
	- Anthropic: Claude 
	- OpenAI: Operator (upcoming agent platform) 
	- Microsoft: Copilot 
	- Capital One: Eno (gender-neutral banking assistant) 
	- Disney: Judy Hopps (character-based bot)
