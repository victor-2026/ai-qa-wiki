## Key posts (2026)

### Post: Goodhart's Law in AI evaluation (hallucination rate example)

Core argument: metrics/KPIs/success criteria are needed to judge if AI does a task "well", but they have limits — Goodhart's Law: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."

Example: measuring hallucination rate of an AI answering from company policy documents. Metric: "how accurately does every statement reflect the source material?" The easiest way to improve the score: say less, stick closely to the wording, avoid making connections between parts of the source, refuse to answer on ambiguity. Hallucination rate goes down, accuracy against source goes up — but the system is not necessarily better. It is better at passing the test. The real question: can the AI give a useful, complete and trustworthy answer based on the information it has?

Conclusion: what we measure and how we measure matters; testers need to go further — this is where AI evaluation gets interesting. Hashtags: #AI #AIEvaluation #AITesting.
