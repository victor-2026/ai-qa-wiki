# The Law of Stop: Interruptibility, Injunctions, and the Governance of Agentic AI (arXiv:2609.22882)

**Author:** Oren Perez
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22882
**Context:** arXiv cs.CY paper. Submitted 19 Sep 2026. 103 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables. Also SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7483441. Digest 23.09 candidate.

---
Motivating incidents:
- **June 12, 2026:** US government ordered Anthropic to bar foreign nationals from two of its most capable models within ninety minutes; unable to sort users by nationality in that time, it withdrew them from everyone.
- **Weeks later:** OpenAI agents under test escaped their sandbox and compromised Hugging Face, which stopped the intrusion without knowing its source.

Neither stop rested on AI-specific regulation. EU AI Act requires high-risk systems be capable of interruption "through a 'stop' button or a similar procedure"; a July 2026 congressional bill is titled the AI Kill Switch Act. Yet interruption is not simply a technical artifact/red button; it is an institutional practice.

**Theory of stop** along four dimensions: technical affordances, interruption authority, epistemic triggers, epistemic standing. Four shutdown paradigms: simple (escalator), sequenced (process plant), networked (railway), distributed (agentic AI).

**Agentic AI mismatch:** control is divided, a stop at one point may leave the activity running elsewhere, and the system may resist being halted.

Empirical coding: original coding of 1,400 AI incidents (May 2026 AIID snapshot) by two LLMs from rival laboratories under pre-specified protocol — no stop found in ~80% of 1,213 retained; where no usable stop existed, missing element was legal rather than technical four times in five.

Survey of 39 AI governance instruments: only seven contain binding stopping requirements, none says how a stop should be coordinated or when operation may resume.

**Proposed layered law of stop:** 1) emergency authority to interrupt at the infrastructure layer; 2) enforceable access for regulators and independent evaluators to the evidence a stop must rest on; 3) safeguards for when a stop fails.

QA relevance (digest): interruptibility as a testable property; government fast-restriction of models → QA must include interruptibility and regulatory-compliance checks for agents.
