# "Slop Coding" and Responsible Testers (Satisfice, 2025-10-30)

**Author:** James Bach
**Source:** https://www.satisfice.com/blog/archives/487969
**Context:** Satisfice blog. Critique of the "vibe coding" euphemism; proposes "slop-coding". Categories: AI and Testing, Automation, Ethics, RST Methodology, RST.

---
"Vibe coding" is cutesy — the term takes digital programming and smears it with feel-good language. Bach uses AI to create one-off test tools for projects, but needs an honest way to say what he's done: "I don't want to simply say 'I created a tool to do x' because maybe they will expect me to stand behind my work, which I am not really in a position to do. As a responsible tester, I need to be clear about what I know and what I don't know."

The solution: **"slop-coding"** — "I slop-coded a tool to detect memory leaks." Signals that the tool has NOT been systematically tested. Once it is tested well, say "I created a tool..."

Comments (Bolton's variants): "slip-coded" (minor mistake), "schlep-coded". John Waterhouse: there will be lots of money unpicking vibe-coded production software; AI lowers the bar to create disposable tools; "people get all excited when things look shiny."

QA relevance: naming the responsibility gap in AI-generated code — honesty about what is and isn't tested. Maps to QA role honesty: quoted in digest around empirical evidence of AI slop in production; ties directly to PRW v2.0 operator rule (must be ready to stand by your work) and to the "seldom-findable artifacts need attestation" theme.
