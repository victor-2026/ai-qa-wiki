Test engineering, leadership, strategy. LinkedIn post (2026-08-22, shared 5d ago from 27 Aug).

Core argument: Testers do much more than try to use the product the same way a user would. Finding critical defects efficiently requires analysis and methods very different than what a real user will do.

Example: A real user is not going to emit an HTTP 503 from a backend service at the same time a cron job is attempting to evaluate the contents of a distributed cache. Yet that might be a test condition conceived by a tester that exposes a critical defect.

Call to action: Keep imagination wide open and less narrowed by cliche ways of describing testing.

Michael Bolton (Rapid Software Testing, context-driven testing school) commented: "Yes, we know how to test AI. Let's have more of us provide rich descriptions of the alternative perspectives."

Context: Part of broader debate about "testing is just using the product" reductive narrative. Wayne + Michael Bolton represent context-driven testing tradition that resists simplified testing descriptions.
